Features

Listen, understand, answer. Locally.

Hirora combines live speech recognition, a small fast language model and your own documents, all running on your computer.

Live transcript

Hirora captures your microphone and what plays through your speakers or headphones as two separate streams, so you always know who said what. It hears the call wherever it plays: Meet in a browser tab, the Teams or Zoom app, or a Bluetooth headset that switches to its hands-free mode.

  • Text appears while people speak and is finalised when they pause.
  • A custom vocabulary teaches it names, products and acronyms.
  • In person? Switch to one microphone for the whole room, and the questions it hears are answered too.
  • Background chatter (“wait a minute”, someone in the room) is recognised as such and doesn't trigger answers.
  • Audio is processed in memory and never written to disk.

Instant answers

When the other side asks a question, Hirora starts writing the answer before they finish speaking and shows it the moment they pause. You can also type a question or paste text from the clipboard, and when a question is about something on screen, Hirora looks at it.

  • Answers read like a person explaining, not a chatbot: connected sentences, no filler.
  • Follow-ups in one click: Expand, Beginner, Concise, Retry.
  • Coding answers include working code and test cases, with a Copy button.

One assistant for everything

There are no modes to pick. Ask in your own words and Hirora works out what kind of answer you need:

You askWhat you get
A question about you or your workAn answer in the first person, built from your résumé and notes. If they don't cover it, Hirora says so.
A coding or design taskThe answer first, then the approach, working code, complexity and test cases.
“What is…”, “How does…”A plain explanation with a short example.
“Summarise”, “What did we decide?”Decisions, action items with owners and open questions, from the transcript so far.
Anything elseA short, direct answer.

In Settings you choose only how answers read: length, language and tone.

Reads the shared screen

When someone shares code, a diagram or a slide and asks about it (“can you add role-based access to this?”, “which of these routes should be admin-only?”), Hirora takes one picture of the screen you're looking at and answers about what's actually on it, using the names it sees. Questions that don't point at the screen skip it and stay as fast as before.

  • Hirora's own windows are left out of the picture.
  • The picture stays in memory, goes only to the answer model on your PC and is discarded after the answer. It is never saved.
  • In our tests on an RTX 4060 laptop (plugged in), a typed question about code shown in a browser started answering in 1.5–3.3 s across our runs, including a 0.5–1.6 s screen capture, and named the routes on screen. A question without the screen started in 0.5–0.7 s.
  • If what you ask about isn't on the screen, it says so instead of guessing.
  • Settings → Answers → Your screen: when a question refers to it (default), with every question, or never. Needs the graphics-card setup; the processor-only model can't read images.

Your documents

Add PDFs, Word files, text, Markdown or code: notes, specs, a job description, a résumé. Hirora indexes them on your PC and uses the most relevant passages in its answers, citing the file and page. If an answer would need a fact that isn't in your materials, it says so instead of inventing one.

Overlay and shortcuts

The overlay is a small translucent window that floats above your call and never takes focus away from it. Drag it anywhere, make it compact, or move it to another monitor.

By default Windows is asked to leave Hirora's windows out of screen shares and screenshots: you see them, a shared screen doesn't. This works with Chrome, Edge and other apps that use Windows' standard capture, but cameras and some remote-desktop tools ignore it, so treat it as a privacy convenience, not invisibility.

Every action has a global shortcut, for example Ctrl Shift Space to show the overlay. The full list is in the guide.

The engine

Hirora installs and runs its own engine: whisper.cpp for speech and an Ollama runtime for answers, both listening only on 127.0.0.1. On first launch it checks your hardware and picks the right models.

With an NVIDIA graphics card (6 GB or more) it uses Whisper large-v3-turbo and Qwen 3.5 4B; elsewhere Whisper small and Qwen 3 1.7B on the processor. See the requirements for measured speeds.

Privacy

No account, no keys, no analytics. Nothing you say, type or add is sent anywhere; the only network use is the one-time engine download. History is off unless you turn it on. Read the privacy page for details.
