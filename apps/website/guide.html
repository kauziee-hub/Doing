Using Hirora

From the first launch to your first call, plus the shortcuts and fixes worth knowing.

First run

After installing (see Download), open Hirora. A short setup walks you through:

  1. Welcome

    What Hirora does and what stays on your PC.

  2. Set up

    Hirora shows your hardware and the four parts it needs: answer engine, speech recognition, speech model and answer model. Press Set up Hirora. You can keep working while it downloads; if it stops, press Resume setup and it continues where it left off.

  3. Your documents

    Optionally add notes or a résumé so answers can use them.

  4. Ready

    The main shortcuts are listed. Start listening from Home or the overlay.

On a call

  1. Join the call in Meet, Teams, Zoom or any other app.
  2. Press Ctrl Shift Space to show the overlay, then the microphone button (or Ctrl Shift L).
  3. Confirm that everyone has agreed to be transcribed. Capture starts only after this.
  4. The transcript appears with Mic (you) and System (the other side) labelled. Questions from the other side are answered automatically; for your own, press Ctrl Shift Enter.
  5. Stop with the same button. Nothing is kept unless you turned on history.

Press Start a minute before the call: the first answer after starting waits while the model loads (about 15–20 seconds on a GPU).

Asking anything

There's one assistant and nothing to switch. Ask out loud, or type with Ctrl Shift K, in your own words:

  • About you (“tell me about your last project”): answered in the first person from your résumé and notes.
  • Code and design (“write a function that…”, “how would you scale this?”): answer, approach, code, complexity and tests.
  • Concepts (“what is RBAC?”): a plain explanation with an example.
  • The call so far (“summarise”, “what are the action items?”): decisions, owners and open questions from the transcript.

Under any answer, Expand, Beginner, Concise and Retry rewrite it in one click.

When someone shares their screen

Questions that point at something shown, like “can you establish an RBAC protocol in this?” or “which of these routes should be admin-only?”, make Hirora take one picture of the monitor you're using and answer about what's on it. The overlay marks those answers with screen. Keep the shared screen visible on that monitor when the question is asked.

For a coding problem that is shared with you, or that you open from a link, press Ctrl Shift O (or the camera button on the overlay): Hirora reads the question on your screen and answers it, with nothing to type or paste. Phrases like “can you solve this?”, “here's the problem” or “open the link I sent” do the same when someone says them. Read the code before you use it: a small model on a laptop gets tricky problems wrong sometimes.

The picture is kept in memory only and sent to the answer model on your PC. Change when it's used in Settings → Answers → Your screen.

Adding documents

Open Knowledge and drop in PDF, Word, text, Markdown, CSV, JSON or code files, and set each one's type (résumé, project notes, job description, notes…). They're parsed and indexed on your PC. Use Test retrieval to see which passages a question would use. Deleting a document removes its text and index.

Under Profiles you can save a profile for a specific company or meeting so answers use the right context.

Settings

  • Engine: what's installed, which hardware is used, transcription language, custom vocabulary, and where the conversation is: on a call, or in a room with one microphone for everyone.
  • Audio: microphone, which output to listen to (all speakers and headphones by default), device tests.
  • Answers: length, language, tone, when to look at your screen, and which sources trigger automatic answers.
  • Overlay & capture: opacity, size, always on top, and hiding Hirora from screen shares.
  • Hotkeys and Privacy & data: shortcuts, history, and deleting your data.
  • Updates: your version, Check for updates, Download and install, and the daily check.

Tips

  • Keep a laptop plugged in. On battery the graphics card slows down sharply and answers take several seconds.
  • Use headphones so your microphone doesn't pick up the other side.
  • Set the language (Settings → Engine) if you always speak the same one; automatic detection is slightly slower.
  • Add a custom vocabulary for names and product terms Whisper might mishear.

Troubleshooting

Setup stopped or failed

Press Resume setup: finished parts are kept and the interrupted download continues. “Failed its checksum” means the file was damaged on the way and was deleted; resume to fetch it again. If it keeps failing, a proxy or antivirus may be altering downloads. Setup needs about 1.6 times the download size free while it unpacks.

Answers or transcripts are slow

Plug the laptop in and press Start a minute before the call. Settings → Engine shows whether the fast (graphics card) or processor setup is in use.

No transcript of the other side

Check that the call's audio plays on this PC (Settings → Audio → Test system audio). The default “All speakers & headphones” follows Bluetooth headsets that switch to hands-free mode.

My microphone is silent

Windows Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone → allow desktop apps, then Settings → Audio → Test microphone.

A shortcut does nothing

Another app owns it. Pick a different one in Settings → Hotkeys.

“The engine did not start”

Restart Hirora; Settings → Engine shows what's missing and Set up reinstalls it. An old NVIDIA driver can stop the graphics-card setup: update the driver or choose Processor only in Settings → Engine.

The answer ignored what was shared

Check Settings → Answers → Your screen isn't set to Never, and that the shared window is on the monitor where your mouse is. Mention it in the question (“this code”, “on the screen”), press Ctrl Shift O, or choose With every question. The processor-only setup can't read the screen.

Where are the logs?

%APPDATA%\Hirora\logs\hirora.log (no transcripts or answers) and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Hirora\ollama\server.log for the answer engine.

Responsible use

Transcribing other people requires their consent in many places: Hirora asks you to confirm it every time you start. Many interviews, exams and assessments don't allow AI assistance; check the rules and respect them. Hiding the overlay from screen shares is a privacy convenience, not a way to conceal AI use from anyone entitled to know.
