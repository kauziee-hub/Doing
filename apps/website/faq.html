FAQ

Questions, answered

Can't find yours? The guide covers setup and troubleshooting in detail.

General

Wasn't this called Hirora?

Yes. From version 0.4.0 it's called Doing. Installing Doing replaces Hirora and keeps your settings, documents and downloaded models.

What is Doing?

A desktop assistant for calls on Windows. It transcribes the call live, answers questions as they come up, and summarises decisions and action items when you ask, using AI models that run on your own computer.

Do I have to pick a mode?

No. There's one assistant. Ask in your own words and it answers in the right shape: about your experience in the first person, code with tests, a plain explanation, or a summary of the call. Settings only control length, language and tone.

Can it see what someone is sharing?

Yes, on PCs with the graphics-card setup. When a question points at something on screen, like “add RBAC to this”, “which of these routes should be admin-only?” or “can you solve this problem?”, Doing takes one picture of the monitor you're using and answers about what's on it. Press Ctrl Shift O to have it answer whatever question is on your screen, with nothing to type. The picture stays in memory and goes only to the model on your PC. Other questions don't use the screen. You can turn it off in Settings → Answers.

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no account, subscription or key. Because everything runs on your PC there's no server to pay for and no usage limit.

Which apps does it work with?

Any call whose audio plays on your PC: Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Webex, Slack huddles, a browser tab or a video. Doing listens to your speakers and headphones, not to the app itself, so nothing needs to be installed in the call app.

Does it work on Mac or Linux?

Not yet. Doing uses Windows audio capture and is tested on Windows 11 (64-bit).

Can I use it in person, with one microphone?

Yes. In Settings → Engine, set Where the conversation is to In a room: the microphone then stands for everyone present, and the questions it hears are answered too. On a call (the default) only the other side's audio is answered automatically; a question you ask yourself is answered when you press Ctrl Shift Enter.

Which languages does it understand?

Whisper recognises many languages and detects them automatically; you can set yours in Settings → Engine for slightly faster results. Answers are written in the language you choose in Settings → Answers. Most testing has been in English.

Privacy

Does anything I say leave my computer?

No. Speech recognition and answers run on your PC. The only internet use is the one-time download of the engine and a daily check for a newer version (which you can turn off). See the privacy page.

Does it record my calls?

No. Audio is transcribed in memory and never written to disk. Transcript history is off unless you turn it on.

Can other people on the call see Doing when I share my screen?

By default Doing asks Windows to leave its windows out of screen shares and screenshots, and that worked with Chrome and Edge screen sharing in our tests. Cameras, some remote-desktop tools and hardware capture ignore it, so don't rely on it.

Speed and hardware

How fast are the answers?

Measured with Doing 0.3.1 on an RTX 4060 laptop, plugged in: a typed question gets its first answer word after a median 0.45 s (0.42–1.32 s over 12 questions), and Whisper transcribes a 2.4–3.5 s spoken question in 0.12–0.20 s. With spoken questions (measured on 0.3.0), when the question was clear before the speaker finished, the answer appeared 0.3 s after they stopped; otherwise it started 1.2–2.7 s after.

A question about the shared screen takes longer because the model reads the picture first: 1.5–3.3 s to the first word with code on screen in our tests with Doing 0.3.1, including a 0.5–1.6 s capture (5.1 s once, with a busier window on screen).

The first question after pressing Start waits about 15–20 s while the model loads, so start a minute before the call.

Do I need a graphics card?

No, but it helps a lot. With an NVIDIA card with 6 GB or more, Doing uses larger models on the card. On other PCs it uses smaller models on the processor: in our test (graphics card switched off, laptop on battery) spoken questions were transcribed correctly and answers started 2.3–3.6 s after the speaker stopped. Reading the screen and screenshots needs the graphics-card setup.

Why is it slow on my laptop?

Most often the laptop is on battery: graphics cards slow down sharply to save power. Plug it in. Settings → Engine shows which setup is in use.

How much does it download?

6.2 GB with an NVIDIA card, 3.2 GB otherwise, once. If you already use Ollama, Doing reuses it and its models.

How good are the answers?

They come from small models (4B or 1.7B parameters) chosen for speed, not from a large cloud model. They're good at explanations, definitions, code and answers grounded in your documents, and they're instructed to say when your materials don't cover something rather than invent it. Check anything important.

Install and use

Why does Windows warn me when I install?

The installer isn't code-signed yet. Compare the SHA-256 on the download page with your file (Get-FileHash .\Doing-Setup-*.exe in PowerShell). If they match, choose More info → Run anyway.

Do I need to be online?

Only for the first setup. After that Doing works without an internet connection; it just can't check for updates.

How do I update Doing?

From 0.4.0 on, Doing checks once a day and tells you when a new version is out: open Settings → Updates and press Download and install. It downloads the installer from GitHub, checks its SHA-256, closes, updates and opens again, keeping your settings, documents and models. On Hirora 0.3.7 or older, quit it and run the Doing installer from the download page once.

Is Doing allowed in interviews?

Often not. Many interviews and assessments forbid AI help, so check the rules first. Doing is meant for meetings, learning, practice and calls where assistance is permitted. Always tell people when you're transcribing them.

Is the source code available?

Yes, on GitHub.
