Privacy

Your calls stay on your PC.

Hirora has no account system, no server and no analytics. Speech recognition and answers run on your computer, so nothing you say, type or add is sent anywhere.

What we collect

Nothing. Hirora doesn't have accounts, keys, telemetry, crash reporting or usage analytics. There is no Hirora server that your audio, transcripts, documents or questions could reach.

Raw audio is never written to disk. Screenshots you take as context are held in memory for up to five minutes while you preview them, then discarded.

Network use

The app connects to the internet only for the one-time engine setup, to download:

  • the Ollama runtime and whisper.cpp from GitHub,
  • the Whisper speech model from Hugging Face,
  • the Qwen answer model from the Ollama library.

These are ordinary file downloads: no account, key or personal data is involved, and each file is checked against its published SHA-256. Hirora's speech and answer engines then run on 127.0.0.1 and accept no connections from other computers.

Audio and screen

  • Nothing is captured until you press Start and confirm the consent prompt. A “Listening” badge shows while capture is on.
  • Your microphone and the call's audio are captured as separate streams and transcribed in memory.
  • Clipboard text and screenshots are used only after you preview them and press Send. Likely passwords and keys are flagged so you can redact them.

Deleting data

  • A document: Knowledge → delete. Its text and index are removed.
  • History: the History page, per session or all at once.
  • Everything: Settings → Privacy & data → Delete all data.
  • The app and its models: uninstall, then delete %APPDATA%\Hirora and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Hirora.

This website

This site is a set of static pages hosted on Vercel, with no cookies, analytics or third-party scripts. The installer is served from Vercel Blob storage. Like any web host, Vercel processes standard request data (such as IP addresses) to deliver pages.

Consent and acceptable use

Transcribing other people requires their consent in many places. Many interviews and assessments forbid AI assistance. Hirora's option to stay out of screen shares is a privacy convenience that some capture methods ignore; it is not a way to hide the use of AI from anyone entitled to know.
