# Changelog

## 0.3.3 — 2026-11-26 (unsigned)
Three fixes found while loading a real set of interview documents (a short résumé, six project write-ups and a company profile) and asking 37 interview questions:
- **Adding documents could make every answer fail.** The prompt overflowed the local model's 4K context ("the prompt is longer than the context length currently available to the model"): an 8,000-character résumé plus three 900-character passages and a filled-in profile. Evidence now has a budget (`EVIDENCE_PROMPT_CHARS` = 7,400 characters): the résumé first, capped at 5,200 so the cached prefix stays the same, then other passages while they fit. If the engine still reports an overflow, the answer retries once with half the evidence.
- **Screen reading triggered on ordinary conversation.** "Where do you see yourself in five years?" captured the screen and answered about what happened to be on it, after 29 s. A capture now needs something shown ("this code", "my screen", "fix this", "which of these routes"); "see yourself", "look at failure", "those projects", "what did you learn from this project" and "how would you improve it" no longer count.
- **Numbers were cut at the decimal point** in long document paragraphs ("0.25 s" became "0." and "25 s", "Avinya 2.0" became "2." and "0"). Sentences now break only at punctuation followed by a space.

## 0.3.2 — 2026-11-24 (unsigned)
For users: a new choice in Settings → Engine, *Where the conversation is*: on a call (as before) or in a room, where one microphone hears everyone and its questions are answered too. When the microphone picks up the call through the speakers (no headphones), the answer model no longer reads that copy as something you said. The model now sees which transcript lines are yours and which are the other side's. Everything else below is an experimental speech path that is off by default. The installer grows from 114 MB to 143 MB because it now carries that path's turn model (SAVA, 26 MB) and runtime.

**Live speech path with SAVA end-of-turn, opt-in** (`stt.streamingTranscription`, off by default). Contributed on the `gowtham` branch, fixed and measured before merging (`docs/STT_STREAMING.md`).
- **What it does:** SAVA (Inworld's end-of-turn model, unofficial Apache-2.0 ONNX export, int8) decides when a turn ends, scoring every 40 ms block in order (4.4 ms per block on one CPU thread here); the energy VAD takes over if it isn't loaded. Live text is LocalAgreement-2 over re-decodes of the whole turn: words two decodes agree on are fixed, the rest shown grey. The final text is one more decode of the whole turn. Turns over 27 s are split.
- **Fixed from the branch** (as pushed it transcribed nothing live: 0 of 3 spoken questions, vs 3 of 3 on 0.3.1): whisper-server runs with `-nt`, so the word times the sliding window cut on were all null and every word was dropped; only every other 20 ms frame reached Whisper; nothing kept the audio from before speech was detected (first word clipped); the end-of-turn decode was skipped when a tick was in flight or less than 0.5 s remained (last words lost); SAVA ran fire-and-forget with a shared state and answered one block late; the energy fallback counted 20 ms frames as 40 ms and ended turns after ~120 ms of silence, and reported the pause mark at the end of the hangover instead of the end of speech; Whisper's prompt held the turn's own words (it then skips them); early answers used committed words only, which lag a tick; the prompt carried the transcript twice; your own spoken question could no longer be answered with Ctrl+Shift+Enter; the SAVA model was fetched from a repository's `main` with no checks (it changed the day the branch was pushed).
- **Why whole-turn decodes, not sliding windows:** measured with the replay below, whisper.cpp takes the same ~0.2 s for 0.5 s or 15 s of audio (it always encodes a 30 s block), and its word times are too rough to cut on: zero-length words, "you" dropped at a cut, a cut mid-word turning "a process" into "the process", and a mostly silent tail decoded as "Thank you.".
- **Measured.** Silent replay (`tests/integration/stt-replay.test.ts`: SAPI TTS fixture into WAV, real whisper-server started like the app, real time): all three questions word for word with SAVA and with the energy VAD. Spoken through the speakers with `tools/stt-trace.mjs` (RTX 4060 laptop on AC; the trace prints which path actually ran — an earlier "live" run quoted in a first draft of this entry had in fact used the 0.3.1 path, and its numbers are withdrawn):
  - **First word lost:** on live loopback audio SAVA took 0.6–0.75 s to call clear speech at the start of some questions (per-block numbers from `HIRORA_STT_DEBUG=1`), past the 500 ms pre-roll, so "Please introduce yourself." became "Introduce yourself." (2 of 2 traces before the fix; never on the replay's clean WAVs). Fixed: a frame the energy VAD hears as loud counts as speech too, so the turn starts as in 0.3.1 and SAVA decides when it ends. After the fix every question came out word for word in 4 of 4 SAVA traces.
  - **End of turn:** SAVA calls it 81–99 ms after the voice stops, the energy VAD 272–290 ms.
  - **Still not faster:** end of speech → final text 259–539 ms (median ~470) on the live path vs 239–513 ms (~410) on 0.3.1, and end of speech → first answer token 1.04–1.46 s (~1.24 s) vs 0.88–1.55 s (~0.93 s), two alternating rounds of three questions. The end of the turn isn't the bottleneck on this PC: a Whisper decode takes 190–380 ms while the answer model shares the GPU (130–140 ms otherwise), and 0.3.1 already decodes the moment the speaker goes quiet and starts early answers from its live text. The live path now does the same (a decode 60 ms into a pause, reused as the final text when it covers the end of speech) and waits for the final text before showing an early answer, but it doesn't beat 0.3.1. So it stays off by default.
- **On for everyone:** transcript lines are labelled `[user]` (your mic) and `[other side]` (call audio) in the prompt; a mic line that copies what the call just said (no headphones) is dropped; Settings → Engine → *Where the conversation is*: on a call, or in a room (one mic for everyone, so its questions are answered too). The spoken check of this default path against the installed 0.3.1 (AC, two runs each, alternating): identical transcripts, p50 915 and 840 ms vs 932 and 868 ms to the first token.
- SAVA model: `tools/fetch-sava-onnx.mjs` pins revision `f27cfec88d28`, 26,457,983 bytes, SHA-256 `107b298a…8e8dd9`, and `pnpm package:win` runs it. Only onnxruntime-node's Windows x64 CPU build is packaged (no macOS, Linux, ARM or DirectML files): installer 143 MB (0.3.1: 114 MB; the branch as pushed: 238 MB). SAVA loads in the packaged app only when the live path is on.
- Tests: `liveCapture.test.ts` (every frame and the pre-roll reach Whisper, pause about 0.3 s after the voice, SAVA in order, a loud start starts the turn even when SAVA is slow, a decode at the start of a pause becomes the final text, the last word kept, final = whole-turn decode), the mic hotkey candidate, null word times; opt-in `HIRORA_STT_REPLAY=1` replay against the real engine.
- Tools: `tools/stt-trace.mjs` speaks three questions and prints, per question, when they played, the system-audio level, every live transcript update, the speech timings and the answer timing; with the live path on, the Performance page shows `stt_decode`, `stt_final_wait` and `turn_end_detect` (timings only).

## 0.3.1 — 2026-11-22 (unsigned)
**One assistant that also reads the shared screen** (ADR 0013).
- **No modes.** Meeting Notes, General Q&A, Explain / Teach, Coding, Interview Practice and Live Copilot are merged into one assistant whose prompt covers every kind of request: questions about the user (first person, from the résumé, missing facts flagged), code and design tasks (answer, approach, code, complexity, tests), concepts, summaries of the call (decisions, owners, open questions), anything else. The mode pickers are gone from Home, the overlay, onboarding, Profiles and Settings; Home shows what the assistant can do instead. A saved mode from 0.3.0 loads as `assistant`. Automatic answers are no longer switched off by a "notes" mode.
- **Reads the shared screen.** When a question points at something shown ("can you establish an RBAC protocol in this?", "which of these routes should only an admin call?", "what does this function do", "on the screen"), `refersToScreen()` matches it and the main process captures the display under the cursor with `desktopCapturer` (1280 px wide JPEG, content protection forced on for Hirora's windows during the capture so they're left out), in parallel with retrieval. The picture goes to the answer model on 127.0.0.1 with the question and is dropped after the answer; it is never written to disk or history. The overlay marks such answers *screen*. Settings → Answers → Your screen: *when the question refers to it* (default), *with every question*, *never*; disabled on the CPU tier, whose model reads no images.
- Measured (dev build, RTX 4060 laptop on AC, code shown full-screen in Edge, `tools/screen-check.mjs`, two runs): screen questions started answering in 1.9–2.4 s ("establish RBAC in this") and 1.6–1.7 s ("which of these routes"), capture included (0.48–0.76 s at 1280×800), and named the routes and handlers on screen; "What is RBAC?" skipped the screen and started in 0.59–0.61 s. Installed 0.3.1, same check: 1.98 s and 1.49 s with the screen (captures 0.47 and 0.68 s), 0.51 s without.
- Requests with an image get a 15 s first-token limit (instead of 30 s), so a rare Ollama stall on the image step is retried on the second slot sooner.
- Questions about the user ("What are your strengths?", "Introduce yourself", "Tell me about a time…") get an explicit first-person line in the prompt: typed, they read like the user asking Hirora, and the merged prompt answered one with "Your main strength is…". Requests to write or change code ("write a function that…", "fix this query", "add RBAC to this" with a screen capture) likewise get a line asking for the code layout: without it the merged prompt answered "Write a function that checks balanced brackets" in prose with no code; with it, **Answer**/**Approach**/**Code**/**Complexity**/**Tests** and a code block (first token 0.48–0.59 s). Installed 0.3.1, `tools/quiet-check.mjs` (AC, two runs): all personal answers in the first person; typed questions p50 0.45 s to the first token (0.42–1.32 s, n=12); Whisper 0.12–0.17 s for 2.4–3.5 s questions once warm.
- With a screen capture attached, the prompt says to admit when what the question refers to isn't visible instead of guessing. Before this line, a run where another window covered the code answered "which of these routes…" with invented routes (`/admin/config`); after it, with a notes page on screen and the answer memory cleared: "I can't see any code or routes on that screen; it's just a team offsite notice…", then a general answer marked as general knowledge.
- `tools/screen-check.mjs` pins its Edge window on top: Windows doesn't let a background process take the foreground, so the window that was active when the check started stayed in front and was captured instead. Final installed build, code in front (two runs): 3.1 and 3.3 s ("establish RBAC in this") and 2.3 and 2.4 s ("which of these routes") to the first token with 1.1–1.6 s captures, naming the routes on screen; "What is RBAC?" 0.60–0.68 s without the screen.
- Settings → Answers is now one card (length, language, tone) plus *Your screen*; History no longer shows a mode per session.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-11-22 (unsigned)
**One engine, on your PC, set up by the app** (ADR 0012). Hirora Cloud and every bring-your-own-key option are gone.
- **One-click setup.** First launch (and Settings → Engine) shows the hardware, the four components and one *Set up Hirora* button. Downloads are pinned to exact URLs, sizes and SHA-256 values, resume after interruptions, and are refused if they don't match; archives are unpacked with Windows' `tar.exe`; the answer model is pulled through Ollama, which checks every layer. A fresh PC (empty folders, no Ollama) downloaded, verified and installed the CPU-tier set of the time (4.4 GB, with `qwen3.5:2b`) in 17 min and answered; swapping one component downloaded only that one.
- **Sized to the PC.** An NVIDIA GPU with 6 GB or more gets whisper.cpp CUDA + large-v3-turbo and `qwen3.5:4b` (6.2 GB); other PCs get the CPU build + Whisper small and `qwen3:1.7b` (3.2 GB; not `qwen3.5:2b`, whose hybrid architecture makes llama.cpp re-read the whole prompt for every question: 4.1–4.5 s to the first token on a CPU vs 0.16–0.18 s). The CPU model reads no images, so screenshots say they need the GPU setup. Settings → Engine can force either.
- **Hirora runs its own Ollama** (`ollama serve` on 127.0.0.1:11435, two parallel slots) so the "is this a question for me?" check runs next to the answer instead of on a cloud server. An Ollama you already installed is reused, models included; if it holds Hirora's model in GPU memory, Hirora asks it to unload it first.
- **Removed:** Hirora Cloud (relay functions deleted; the Vercel project now only serves the website, `apps/cloud` → `apps/hosting`), Gemini, xAI, DeepSeek and custom endpoints, cloud transcribers, API-key storage, Gemini embeddings, budgets and prices, the provider benchmark (`pnpm perf:live`), `cloud.e2e.ts` and `live.e2e.ts`.
- **Upgrading from 0.2.x keeps your setup**: settings schema 2 drops only the removed sections. Old encrypted API keys are no longer read; *Delete all data* removes them.
- Cleaner screens: onboarding is Welcome → Set up → Your résumé → Ready; Settings tabs are Engine, Audio, Answers, Overlay & capture, Hotkeys, Privacy & data; Home shows the setup card until the engine is ready, and Start is disabled until then.
- Measured speed (installed 0.3.0, RTX 4060 laptop on AC): median 0.55 s from a typed question to the first answer word (0.50–1.97 s, 12 questions); Whisper large-v3-turbo transcribes a 2.4–3.5 s spoken question in 0.14–0.25 s; with spoken questions (TTS through the speakers), the early answer appeared 0.30 s after the speaker stopped; answers that waited for the final transcript started 1.2–2.7 s after. On the processor only (all GPUs hidden, laptop on battery): spoken questions transcribed exactly with Whisper small and answered 2.3–3.6 s after the speaker stopped; typed questions 0.41–0.60 s once warm. Answers asked while the engine is still loading now wait for it instead of failing (the first question after launch takes 16–19 s).
- On a PC without a usable GPU, answers start from the final transcript (no early answers from interim text, which was slow and misheard on a CPU) and prompts leave out earlier answers.
- New tools: `tools/engine-setup-check.mjs` (a fresh PC's setup with real downloads) and `tools/site-shots.mjs` (website screenshots from a fresh profile). `tools/interview-check.mjs` reports the engine tier and warm-up.

## 0.2.4 — 2026-11-22 (unsigned)
- **Near-instant answers: usually the first words appear 0.3–0.4 s after the interviewer stops** (local mode on an NVIDIA GPU). Installed app, RTX 4060 laptop on AC, SAPI-spoken questions through the speakers, measured from the last voiced frame to the first answer token. Last two runs: 305, 2112, 848 / 413, 302, 304 ms; answers revealed at the pause paint ~25–50 ms after their token. Slower cases are questions whose final words arrive late or that pause mid-sentence; the worst seen was 2.1 s. Before: 2–3 s on the cloud. (Earlier figures for answers shown at the final transcript were measured from transcript arrival; `questionEndedAt` now uses the end of speech.)
  - Utterances on local Whisper end after 300 ms of silence (`speechEndHangoverMs`). The pause still fires when the utterance ends on the same frame.
  - **Whisper on this PC** (`local-whisper`): whisper.cpp's CUDA server with `ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0` in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Hirora\whisper`, started and stopped by the app on 127.0.0.1:8178. It re-transcribes the utterance every 350 ms and when the speaker goes quiet, giving live text (a 4 s question takes ~150 ms on the GPU).
  - **Speculative answers**: `AnswerEngine.prefetch/commit/dropPrefetch`. Once the live text reads as a question, the answer starts hidden. It is revealed 300 ms into the speaker's pause (`SttStream.onPause`), with whatever is already generated. The final transcript keeps it if it is the same question (`sameQuestion`), otherwise it is replaced. Unfinished fragments ("Can you tell me …") are never revealed and are joined to what follows.
  - **Pre-read prompt for local models**: the résumé sits first, in a fixed order, and the model reads instructions, profile and résumé once when listening starts (`prime()`). Each answer then only processes the new question. Prompts stay inside Ollama's 4K context (≤8,000 résumé characters, 4 transcript lines, 2 previous answers, ≤480-token first answers).
  - When answers run locally, the question check runs on Hirora Cloud: Ollama serves one request at a time, so a check there queued behind the answer being written (5.7 s). Clear spoken requests ("Please introduce yourself") skip the check.
  - Ollama stays loaded while listening, refreshed every 4 min.
- **Questions that assume facts your materials don't have** (an internship, a thesis, certifications, awards, a GPA, managing people…) carry an explicit "not in the user's materials" note, so small models say "I haven't done a formal internship yet, but …" instead of inventing one.

## 0.2.3 — 2026-11-22 (unsigned)
- **Background talk no longer triggers answers, and casual questions aren't missed.** Every line from the other side is triaged before the answer engine sees it, because a new answer cancels the one on screen. Filler and chatter ("Wait a minute", "Hey Rahul, come here", "Thank you.") are ignored. Questions clearly aimed at the candidate or clearly interview-style are answered at once. Everything in between goes to a one-word ANSWER/SKIP check by the answer model, with the recent transcript and the candidate's name: "What's happening?", "Can you hear me okay?", casual or ungrammatical questions, reactions to noise. If the check fails or takes over 2.5 s, the old score decides. Live run with real background talk: all chatter and logistics skipped, both real questions answered (first token 0.6–0.9 s; check 0.4–0.8 s when used).
- **Answers sound like a person explaining, not a chatbot.** Connected paragraphs with real depth (what, why, how, where, an example), no chatbot phrases, no bullets/headings/bold unless steps or code are asked for. New defaults: paragraph format, medium length, 900 output tokens. Coding answers keep their labelled sections and code block.
- **Questions about the candidate see the whole résumé** (up to 12 chunks), not just its top. "What tech stack did you use in your last internship?" was answered from the wrong part of the résumé before; now the model sees there was no internship and says so.
- The data-tag filter also neutralises `<latest>`. `tools/interview-check.mjs --script chatter` mixes background talk with questions.

## 0.2.2 — 2026-11-22 (unsigned)
- **Hears the other side wherever the call plays.** New default system-audio source, "All speakers & headphones": the helper loops back every active output endpoint and mixes them (`deviceId: "all"`, `CaptureTarget::SystemAll`). It reopens when endpoints appear or disappear. A Bluetooth headset switching to its hands-free endpoint, or Meet playing to a non-default device, no longer silences the interviewer. Measured with OnePlus Buds 4 and their hands-free mic open: a spoken question reached the mix at -8 dBFS.
- **Main window hidden from screen sharing too.** "Hide Hirora from screen sharing and screenshots" now covers the dashboard as well as the overlay. `capture.e2e.ts` checks both against a real Chrome 157 `getDisplayMedia` share.
- **More interviewer phrasings count as questions**, many of them requests without a "?": "I'd like to hear about…", "Let's talk about…", "Please introduce yourself", "give me a brief introduction about yourself" after a greeting, and wh-clauses mid-sentence ("…what is the difference between a process and a thread"). Taken from real Whisper output; statements like "Thank you." stay non-questions.
- `tools/interview-check.mjs`: speaks interviewer questions into a copy of a real profile and reports transcripts, auto-answers and latency. `tools/bt-route-check.mjs`: shows which outputs carry audio while a Bluetooth mic is open.

## 0.2.1 — 2026-11-21 (unsigned)
- **The overlay is hidden from screen sharing by default.** You see it; a Meet/Teams/Zoom screen share (and screenshots) leave it out, via Windows' capture exclusion (ADR 0008). Measured with a real `getDisplayMedia` screen share in Edge 158 and Chrome 157 (new `capture.e2e.ts`). Existing profiles keep their setting: turn it on in Settings → Overlay & capture → "Hide the overlay from screen sharing and screenshots". Best-effort: cameras, some remote-desktop and hardware capture never honour it.
- The "Question end → first answer paint" metric is recorded only while the overlay is visible. A hidden overlay used to report 2–2.5 s "paints" that never reached the screen; with the overlay visible the answer paints 25–73 ms after its first token.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-11-21 (unsigned)
- **Hirora Cloud**, the new default: a free, always-on relay (Vercel Functions in `apps/cloud`, `https://hirora-one.vercel.app/v1`) to Groq's open models (`openai/gpt-oss-20b`, `gpt-oss-120b`) and Whisper. Friends install Hirora, paste the shared access code, and get answers and transcription with no key, no GPU and no cost. The relay checks the code in constant time, allowlists models, caps request and output size, rate-limits per IP and strips account ids from upstream errors (ADR 0010).
- New `cloud-whisper` transcriber, the default for new installs. The local VAD cuts each utterance, sends it as a 16 kHz WAV, and drops silence and segments Whisper marks as no speech. There's no interim text.
- Migration from VeyraCue now waits when either profile is in use (Chromium `lockfile` held) instead of copying a live profile.
- Website deployed at https://hirora-one.vercel.app, with the installer hosted on Vercel Blob, new screenshots of a fresh cloud install and measured cloud latency.
- e2e: new opt-in `cloud.e2e.ts` (fresh profile, access code only). Paint flags stop Chromium's occlusion tracking from hanging screenshots.

First 0.2.0 build (2026-11-19):
- **Renamed VeyraCue → Hirora** (an interview copilot), with a new mark: a white "h" whose shoulder is a rising arch, plus the pink cue dot. App id `app.hirora.desktop`, installer `Hirora-Setup-<v>-x64-unsigned.exe`, helper `hirora-audio.exe`, env vars `HIRORA_*`.
- First launch copies an existing `%APPDATA%\VeyraCue` profile (keys, settings, documents, history) to `%APPDATA%\Hirora` before Electron loads `Local State`, so DPAPI-encrypted keys keep working. The old folder is left untouched.
- **Local answers with Ollama**: new "Ollama (runs on this PC)" provider, with no key, loopback-only endpoint, models listed from the local server, reasoning off for fast cues, and the model preloaded (kept for 30 min) when listening starts.
- `pnpm perf:live` can bench a local model (`HIRORA_BENCH_OLLAMA_MODEL`, `HIRORA_BENCH_PROVIDERS`, `HIRORA_BENCH_SKIP_STT`); the live e2e suite exercises the profile migration and, with `HIRORA_LIVE_OLLAMA_MODEL`, local answers end to end.
- New install website in the style of the brief: hero with a Higgsfield-generated photo, download card, measured stats, local-model section, FAQ, social card. `HIRORA_DOWNLOAD_URL` points downloads at a release host.
